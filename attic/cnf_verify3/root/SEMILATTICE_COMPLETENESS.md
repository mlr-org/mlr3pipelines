# Initial unit propagation, domain consistency and finite semilattices

Author: root, 2026-09-06. This is a new proof route, separate from the cache,
resolution and HLA arguments. It concerns the unchanged source through
`R/CnfFormula_simplify.R:525`, before the subset matrices are allocated.
The [independent review](../semilattice_review/REVIEW.md) is complete and
supports the three claims. Evidence is generated by
`semilattice_checks.py` and the deliberately early-returning private copy in
`semilattice_bridge.R`; the full-kernel result is also checked unchanged.

## Contract and result

Use the ordinary canonical finite-domain contract from the main semantic
review: each domain is a nonempty finite set; clauses have distinct symbols
and nonempty proper ranges; names and values are ordinary supported strings;
the universe is fixed. Constants and the empty conjunction are separate
base cases. Runtime and representability qualifications from the totality
review still apply.

For each symbol `s`, choose any meet operation on its domain `D_s`: an
associative, commutative, idempotent operation. Write `a <= b` when
`meet(a,b)=a`. A range is **closed under meet** if it contains every meet of
two of its values. A range is a **downward ideal** here if it contains every
value below any of its members; this term does not additionally require
upward directedness.

Assume every range is closed under its symbol's meet and every clause has
at most one range that is not a downward ideal. Then:

1. Initial unit propagation returns FALSE exactly when the formula is
   unsatisfiable. No SSE, subsumption matrix or HLA inference is needed for
   this recognition result.
2. Otherwise let `E_s` be the surviving effective unit domain, or `D_s` if
   there is no surviving unit on `s`. The assignment
   `x_s = meet(E_s)` satisfies the original formula and is its least model
   in the chosen product order.
3. Independently of the meet assumptions, initial propagation computes
   exactly the greatest generalized arc-consistent subdomains of the
   original unary-range disjunctions, or recognizes an empty domain.

The full simplifier has the same satisfiability decision on this class by
its separate soundness theorem. These statements do not say that the full
simplifier preserves the syntactic class of each individual clause.

## 1. An exact characterization of an individual proper clause

The relation defined by a proper clause is closed under coordinatewise meet
if and only if all its literal ranges are meet closed and at most one is not
a downward ideal. This characterizes an individual clause, not every CNF
presentation of a meet-closed relation.

Necessity of individual range closure: if `a,b` belong to one range but
their meet does not, fix every other coordinate to a value outside its
range. Both resulting assignments satisfy the clause, while their meet
does not. Properness supplies the outside values.

Necessity of at most one nonideal: for two such ranges choose `a<=b` with
`b` inside and `a` outside the first, and `c<=d` with `d` inside and `c`
outside the second. The assignments `(b,c)` and `(a,d)`, with every other
coordinate fixed outside its range, both satisfy the clause; their meet
`(a,c)` falsifies it.

For sufficiency, take two satisfying assignments. If either satisfies an
ideal literal, their coordinatewise meet still satisfies that literal by
downward closure. Otherwise both must satisfy the sole nonideal literal;
the meet stays in that range by its closure. The conjunction of such
relations is consequently meet closed as well.

## 2. Independent domain-propagation specification

Start with all domains `E_s=D_s`. For each original clause, inspect its
possible literals, those with `R_s intersect E_s` nonempty:

- If there are none, report contradiction.
- If exactly one remains, replace that symbol's domain by
  `E_s intersect R_s`.
- With at least two possible literals, make no change.

Repeat until no domain changes. Finite strict set decreases give
termination. The result is independent of the fair processing order: every
restriction is monotone as a function of the other domains, and every
common fixed point below the initial domains remains below every iterate.
The terminating result is therefore the greatest common fixed point.

These are precisely the generalized arc-consistency restrictions for a
disjunction of unary ranges. A value of one coordinate has support as soon
as another possible literal exists, because that other coordinate can be
chosen inside its range. If there is only one possible literal, its symbol
must use its range, while every other coordinate is unconstrained by that
clause. A clause already satisfied by an entire current domain remains so
under further domain restriction and contributes no new restriction.

The phrase greatest fixed point describes the case with all domains
nonempty. On a detected contradiction we return a single bottom/failure
state, without claiming to preserve a particular vector of empty domains.

## 3. Why this exact source prefix implements that specification

At this phase `is_not_subset_of` stays NULL, so `register_unit` cannot take
the later cached-comparison shortcut. Initially it intersects all original
units, retains one representative per symbol and stores exactly their
effective intersection in `unit_domains` (source 87–136, 483–488).

During the subsequent width-ordered preprocessing loop, a processed live
nonunit is registered under each of its current symbols. Every later unit
registration immediately visits the saved registry for its symbol and
restricts those clauses by the current effective domain. Deleting an empty
literal either leaves a nonunit or immediately registers the resulting
unit; contradiction is forwarded. A clause whose literal contains the
entire restricting domain is deleted as already satisfied. These are
exactly the possibilities in the independent specification.

An unprocessed clause need not yet appear in any registry: its own
preprocessing turn first intersects every symbol with the then-current
effective units (499–510). The captured list of these symbols causes no
gap. In this pre-matrix phase, restricting the current nonunit can produce
a recursive propagation call only after that clause itself has become a
unit. It cannot remain an unregistered nonunit while some callback creates
a newly missed restricting symbol. Once it is a unit, its domain is in the
unit registry and its propagation obligations are handled there.

At the completed boundary every live nonunit is restricted by every
effective unit, has at least two nonempty literals and is not already
satisfied by an entire effective domain. Every live unit agrees with its
effective domain. Clauses deleted earlier remain satisfied by an effective
domain, which only shrinks. Thus the retained state satisfies all original
clause domain-consistency obligations. Each performed update is one of the
specification's sound monotone restrictions, so its result is the greatest
fixed point, not just some fixed point. False exits agree with the failure
state. Later cached-unit scheduling gaps do not affect this prefix result.

The greatestness claim needs more than preservation of complete models.
Explicitly retain each current clause's original identity: when it becomes
a unit, every other literal of that original clause is already impossible
under the then-current effective domains. Any common GAC sub-box below those
domains must therefore obey the remaining literal. This birth certificate
shows that each registration preserves **every** common GAC sub-box below
the initial domains, including boxes containing unsupported values. Satisfied
clause deletion and duplicate-unit intersection retain their corresponding
certificates because effective domains only shrink. Together with boundary
quiescence this proves greatestness without confusing GAC boxes with sets
of full models.

## 4. Constructing the least model

Each initial domain is meet closed. Units and their intersections remain
meet closed, since every initial range is closed and intersections preserve
closure. Before the matrix phase an actual range is only intersected with
these effective domains; no union-based restriction has occurred.

If propagation does not return FALSE, all effective domains are nonempty.
Every finite nonempty meet-closed set `E_s` contains a least value
`e_s=meet(E_s)`. Intersecting an ideal of `D_s` with `E_s` produces an ideal
in the induced order on `E_s`. Every nonempty such ideal contains `e_s`.

Consider any original clause at the domain fixed point. If only one of its
literals is possible, its effective domain is contained in that literal's
range, so the tuple `e` satisfies it. If at least two are possible, at least
one of them is an ideal literal. Its nonempty intersection with `E_s`
contains `e_s`, so the tuple again satisfies the clause. Zero possible
literals would already have caused FALSE. Therefore `e` satisfies every
original clause. This argument can use the original clauses directly;
it does not need to infer the shape of any later simplification output.

All original models survive the sound domain restrictions, so any model
`y` has `y_s in E_s`, hence `e_s<=y_s` for all symbols. Thus `e` is the least
model, including symbols not mentioned by the formula.

## 5. Ordered domains, Boolean Horn and limitations

On a finite total order with meet=min, every unary range is meet closed and
the nonempty proper ideals are precisely prefixes. The test reduces to:
in each clause all but at most one range are prefixes. Each symbol may use
its own chosen ordering; that ordering need not match the vector storage
order. Boolean Horn CNF is the two-value special case, with positive
singletons as heads. Choosing the two-value order independently per symbol
also includes renamable Horn formulas.

Nonchain domains are included. For example, bit masks with meet=bitwise AND
form a finite meet semilattice; arbitrary meet-closed heads and downward
ideals satisfy the theorem. No total ordering that respects all these
relations need exist.

The effective domains need not equal the projections of all models. With
Boolean min order, `(X=0 or Y=1) and (X=0 or Y=0)` belongs to the class.
Initial propagation leaves both domains unchanged, even though no model
has `X=1`. Its least tuple `(0,0)` is nevertheless a model. This excludes a
stronger claim of complete value pruning.

Outside the class, the four two-variable Boolean clauses representing all
sign choices have no initial units and are jointly unsatisfiable. The
prefix leaves them unchanged; the later simplifier recognizes FALSE. Thus
the general domain-consistency theorem alone does not imply complete
satisfiability recognition.

## Background and attribution

The algebraic tractability principle is established CSP theory, not a new
claim of this investigation. Bodirsky, Macpherson and Thapper explain the
finite-domain link between semilattice operations and arc consistency in
Section 2 of [their primary research paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.6616).
The contribution here is the direct characterization for this clause
language, the independent domain specification, and its correspondence to
this particular source prefix. The source proof above is self-contained
and does not assume the implementation is correct because a general CSP
result exists.

Both R 3.6.3 and R 4.6.1 passed the same 12,000 inputs, with identical
counts and saved witness payloads. The portfolio contains every multiset of
one through three permitted clauses on three Boolean symbols (1,539), every
such multiset on two ternary chains (6,544), 2,000 additional random chain
inputs, 1,000 random bit-mask semilattice inputs, three constant/empty cases,
the explicit unsupported-value Horn control, and 913 other-class controls.
The latter also contain some members of the proved class; membership is
computed independently rather than inferred from a generator label.

All 12,000 prefix domain results equal the separate fixed-point computation.
All prefix and full results pass the decision-diagram equivalence oracle
(24,000 comparisons), and 162 selected comparisons are independently checked
with ordinary one-hot Boolean SAT. Exactly 11,456 inputs satisfy the stated
meet-closed-clause conditions: 9,687 are satisfiable and supply checked least
models, and all 1,769 contradictory inputs return FALSE already in the
prefix. Explicitly enumerated original models supply 26,183 additional
coordinatewise least-model checks. Four other-class contradictions remain
unrecognized by initial propagation, as expected.

The local clause characterization was separately checked on all 484 proper
two-literal relations formed from chains of sizes two/three and the
four-element bit-mask diamond, testing 47,459 pairs of satisfying tuples.
This is evidence for the arbitrary-arity proof, not an exhaustive test of
all finite semilattices. Exact result files are `semilattice_r36.json` and
`semilattice_r46.json`; full invocations are in their matching logs.

The independent reviewer additionally checked 34,210 relation-closure cases,
including every labeled semilattice through size four, and 3,791 source-prefix
cases on each R version. Its relational oracle materializes satisfying tuples
